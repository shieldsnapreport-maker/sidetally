How Much Should I Charge Per Hour as a Freelancer? Find Your True Hourly Rate

By the SideTally team · Updated September 30, 2026

Most freelancers price by picking a number that "feels right" or copying what others charge. The problem: the hourly rate on your invoice isn't what you keep per hour. To answer how much should I charge per hour, first find your true hourly rate.

Rate on paper vs. true hourly rate

Your rate on paper is income ÷ hours billed. Your true hourly rate also counts three things the invoice ignores:

  • Unbilled hours: admin and bookkeeping, proposals and sales calls, unpaid revisions, learning, and setup.
  • Business expenses: software, platform and payment fees, and other costs.
  • Tax set-aside: the share of profit you put aside for tax.

True hourly rate = (income − expenses − tax set-aside) ÷ (billed hours + unbilled hours).

A worked example

These are the example numbers that come pre-filled in the free calculator (one month):

InputExample
Income$4,000
Hours billed60
Unbilled hours (admin 8, sales 10, revisions 6, other 4)28
Expenses (software $120, fees $200, other $80)$400
Tax set-aside25%
  • Rate on paper: $4,000 ÷ 60 = $66.67/hr
  • Profit before tax: $4,000 − $400 = $3,600. Tax set-aside (25%): $900. Take-home: $2,700.
  • Total hours: 60 + 28 = 88. True hourly rate: $2,700 ÷ 88 ≈ $30.68/hr, less than half the rate on paper.

Work backwards to the rate you should charge

Say you want to take home $50 for every hour you work. With the same 88 hours, that's $4,400 take-home. Before a 25% set-aside, you need $5,866.67 in profit, plus $400 of expenses: $6,266.67 of income. Spread over 60 billed hours, that's about $104.44 per billed hour.

You don't have to raise your rate that far in one step. Cutting unbilled hours (fewer revision rounds, a clear scope) moves the number too, and the calculator lets you test both.
