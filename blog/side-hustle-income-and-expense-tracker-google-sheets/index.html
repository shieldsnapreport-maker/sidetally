Side Hustle Income and Expense Tracker in Google Sheets: What to Track

By the SideTally team · Updated September 30, 2026

If your side hustle money lives in three apps, a payout email, and your memory, you can't answer a simple question: what did I actually keep this month? A basic side hustle income and expense tracker in Google Sheets answers that in one place. Here's what to put in it.

1. An income log: one row per payment

Every time money comes in, add one row. These seven columns cover almost every side hustle:

ColumnWhy it matters
DateLets you total by month for your P&L.
Client / sourceShows which clients or products earn the most.
PlatformDirect, Upwork, Etsy, Gumroad… fees differ by platform.
AmountThe gross payment before fees.
HoursNeeded for your effective hourly rate.
FeePlatform or payment-processing fee.
NetA formula: =Amount − Fee.

2. An expense log: category, amount, deductible?

Log every business cost with a date, a category, the amount, and a simple Yes/No for "tax-deductible". Common categories: software and subscriptions, supplies, stock assets, contractor costs, a share of your phone and internet, and travel. Which costs count as deductible depends on where you live, so check with a tax professional. The tracker just keeps the list tidy.

3. A monthly profit and loss (P&L)

A P&L tab turns the two logs into one row per month: income − expenses = net. In Google Sheets, SUMIFS can total each month from the logs, so you never type the same number twice. After a few months you'll see which months are slow and plan for them.

4. Your effective hourly rate

Divide net profit by hours worked. This is the number that tells you whether a gig is worth keeping. A $500 project that took 25 hours pays less per hour than a $200 one that took 4.

5. A tax set-aside habit

Pick a percentage of profit to move aside each month, and let the sheet calculate it. The right percentage depends on your situation. The point is the habit, so tax time isn't a surprise. (This is planning, not tax advice.)
